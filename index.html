Conference Overview

Bridging Cognitive Foundations & Real-World Decisions

The School of Psychology and the Department of Behavioral Science at the University of East Anglia (UEA) invite submissions for this joint interdisciplinary conference on November 28–29, 2026.

Modern societal challenges—from climate transition and public health dilemmas to AI-mediated decision architectures and financial resilience—demand integrative approaches that transcend departmental boundaries. This symposium brings together cognitive psychologists, behavioral scientists, experimental economists, neuroscientists, and public policy practitioners.

Over two intensive days in Norwich, participants will share rigorous empirical findings, computational models, novel laboratory and field experiments, and evidence-informed interventions. We particularly welcome contributions from Early Career Researchers (ECRs) and PhD candidates, with dedicated interactive panels and mentorship sessions.

Interdisciplinary Dialogue

Fostering deep synergy between psychological science and behavioral economic theory.

Submissions Scope

Conference Tracks & Topics of Interest

We invite paper, symposium, and poster submissions across the full spectrum of behavioral and psychological sciences.

Track 01

Cognition, Judgement & Choice Architecture

  • Cognitive mechanisms and heuristics in decision-making
  • Perception of risk, probability weighting, and ambiguity
  • Bounded rationality and cognitive modeling
  • Emotion, affect, and visceral influences on judgment
  • Temporal discounting and intertemporal trade-offs
Chair: Cognitive & Experimental Psychology Unit
Track 02

Applied Behaviour, Nudges & Public Policy

  • Behavioral interventions in health, wellness & nutrition
  • Pro-environmental actions & climate change decision-making
  • Financial decision-making, consumer debt & savings behavior
  • Digital choice architectures, algorithmic nudges & AI trust
  • Combating online misinformation and epistemic vigilance
Chair: Behavioural Science & Policy Initiative
Track 03

Social Dynamics, Norms & Experimental Methods

  • Social identity, peer conformity, and social norm transmission
  • Cooperation, public goods, and institutional trust
  • Methodological innovations: lab, field & online experiments
  • Replicability, open science practices, and pre-registration
  • Neuroeconomics and physiological measurements
Chair: Social Psychology & Quantitative Methods
Submission Guidelines

How to Submit Your Work

Please review the submission formats and author requirements carefully.

Format A

Oral Presentation

20-minute talk + 10 minutes moderated audience discussion. Ideal for completed empirical projects or mature theoretical models.

Format B

Thematic Symposium

90-minute session featuring 3–4 coherent papers under a shared umbrella, led by a designated chair and discussant.

Format C

Poster Session

Presented during dedicated evening reception. Excellent for works-in-progress, registered reports, and ECR research.

Submission Checklist & Requirements

1
Extended Abstract Length: Maximum 800 words (excluding title, keywords, references, and up to 1 summary table/figure).
2
Required Structure: Submissions must clearly state:
  • Background & Theoretical Motivation
  • Methodology & Experimental Design (sample sizes, stimuli, procedure)
  • Key Findings / Statistical Results (or anticipated for works in progress)
  • Theoretical & Policy Implications
3
Anonymisation for Blind Review: All papers undergo double-blind peer review. Please eliminate author names, institutions, and identifying self-citations in the text.
4
File Format: Single PDF document (12pt font, 1.5 line spacing, standard margins).
